BTCUSDT memory law and finite-state realization

Data: 14,464,973 BTCUSDT spot aggregate trades, 1,114,521 seconds (~12.90 d). Total trade counts were binned at 1 s. The observed activity ACF was evaluated on 145 logarithmically distributed positive lags from 1 s to 6 h. Errors below are RMSE of log predicted ACF vs log empirical ACF; for small errors this is approximately RMS relative error.

Scope. This identifies the shape of the observed activity-memory curve. It is not yet the final latent covariance C(h): raw count covariance still mixes endogenous Hawkes excitation and exogenous/latent activity.

Can nonlinear recurrence beat exponentials?

For one isolated event, a shifted power kernel q(t)=(1+t/tau)^(-gamma) obeys the exact scalar nonlinear ODE q' = -(gamma/tau) q^(1+1/gamma). But for a superposition x=sum_i q_i, x' depends on sum_i q_i^(1+1/gamma), not on x alone. Introducing moments M_p=sum_i q_i^p creates the hierarchy M_p' = -(p gamma/tau) M_(p+1/gamma), which does not close at finite order without an approximation. Thus the single-event nonlinear recurrence does not give an exact one-state aggregate convolution.

A learned nonlinear recurrent state remains a legitimate approximate compressor. The fair target is therefore not kernel-value fitting alone but operator error over many event histories: generate/hold out histories u, compute exact convolution y=K*u, train a d-state recurrence z_{t+1}=F(z_t,u_t), yhat=G(z_t), and compare its held-out operator error against a d-state optimized linear realization. This avoids claiming compression from a recurrence that only reproduces the decay of one event.

Accuracy floor and what “sub-1%” should mean

A rough 12-block standard-error calculation gives ~0.107 RMS uncertainty in log-ACF across the selected lags; a split-half discrepancy is even larger (~0.44/sqrt(2) per half-comparison scale), reflecting strong nonstationarity at long lags. These are crude diagnostics rather than formal confidence intervals, but they are far above 0.01. Therefore fitting the empirical ACF to 0.1–1% would mostly fit realization-specific fluctuations.

The useful sub-1% criterion is: after choosing/estimating a smooth latent law K(t;theta), require the numerical realization to satisfy ||Khat-K|| (or, better, held-out convolution-operator error) below 1%. Fractional kernels are nonlocal; published fast fractional solvers commonly obtain tractability by rational/sum-of-exponential compression of the history kernel.
