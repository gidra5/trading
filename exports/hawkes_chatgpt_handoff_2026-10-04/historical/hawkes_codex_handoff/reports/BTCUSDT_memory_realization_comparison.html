Interpretation

Optimizing poles/scales matters much more than merely making a dense fixed logarithmic grid. Four optimized positive exponential modes match the empirical kernel about as well as ten fixed-grid modes. The fourth optimized mode is effectively constant over the 6 h fitting window, indicating a slower-than-observed-window component; this should be constrained/tested on longer data before interpreting it physically.

A general stable rational LTI realization with distinct real poles is algebraically the same exponential-sum class after partial fractions. It can add repeated or complex poles, but for this monotone positive covariance target those are not automatically advantageous. Polynomial/Taylor approximations are not competitive as online memory states over a multi-decade lag interval because they do not forget and are poorly conditioned globally. Fractional/power-law laws are parsimonious structurally, but exact online evaluation remains nonlocal and normally requires compressed history or an auxiliary finite-state approximation.
